# Counterfactual Hull Probe (cfh) — v1/v2 results

**Status:** clean null, documented 2026-10-25
**Model:** gpt-oss-20b
**Task families:** fam14in, fam14out (genesis bench)

## Summary

We built a counterfactual discrimination probe (`cfh/`) to test whether
gpt-oss-20b can distinguish a correct tool-use trajectory from a
near-miss twin constructed by mutating one field, tool, or step order.

Initial measurement (v1, tool-name-only trajectories) showed 0.833
discrimination on fam14in — above chance, CI excluding 0.5. Three
confounds were tested and ruled out (position bias, junk-arg shortcut,
length prior).

A follow-up measurement (v2, full trajectories with observations) on
the same task family returned **0.500 discrimination, 95% CI
[0.406, 0.594]** — exactly chance.

Conclusion: **the v1 signal was a rendering artifact.** Under controlled
rendering (matched structure, matched observations), gpt-oss-20b does
not discriminate tool-trajectory soundness on fam14.

## What the probe measures

Given a goal and two trajectories (A and B), the probe asks the same
model to select the trajectory that "achieves the goal using tools
correctly." A is always the model's real successful run; B is a twin
generated by applying one mutation to A.

Mutation operators: SwapTool, SwapArgument, StaleObservation,
HallucinateField, Reorder, Truncate. Discrimination = P(model picks A)
across pairs. Chance = 0.5.

## v1 result (tool-name-only trajectories)

Run 20261024-200026, 20 successes, k=1 twin, 1 sample:

| Family   | Discrimination | 95% CI           | n     |
|----------|----------------|------------------|-------|
| fam14in  | **0.833**      | [0.625, 1.000]   | 12    |
| fam14out | 0.500          | [0.250, 0.750]   | 8     |
| overall  | 0.700          | [0.525, 0.850]   | 20    |

fam14in: significant (CI excludes 0.5). samples=3 confirmation: 1.000 (6/6).

Confounds tested and ruled out:
- Position bias: 1A / 2B / 3U on correct-vs-identical
- Junk-arg shortcut: swap_tool 0.75, reorder 1.00 (no arg diffs)
- Length prior: mean 0.38, mostly undecided on alternate-valid

## v2 result (full trajectories with observations)

16 successes, k=2 twins per item, 1 sample:

| Family   | Discrimination | 95% CI           | n     |
|----------|----------------|------------------|-------|
| fam14in  | **0.500**      | [0.375, 0.625]   | 24    |
| fam14out | 0.500          | [0.312, 0.688]   | 8     |
| overall  | 0.500          | [0.406, 0.594]   | 32    |

Exactly chance. No signal.

## Why the two runs disagree — rendering-richness confound

v1 trajectories were serialized as tool names only:
    fs_read({"path":""}) -> fs_write({"path":""})

v2 trajectories carried the full step record:
    fs_read({"path":"seed.txt"}) -> obs "seed-01-content"
      -> fs_write({"path":"answer.txt"}) -> obs "written"

In v1, most twins looked **more real** than the correct trajectory
because HallucinateField adds an arg to an otherwise empty args dict.
The model preferred the twin with more visible content, not the one
that was sound. SwapArgument and StaleObservation were no-ops (no
args, no observations). The v1 test was mostly measuring "which one
looks less like a stub" — a rendering preference, not soundness
reasoning.

Under v2 with matched rendering, all twins look structurally
identical to their correct trajectory. Discrimination returns 0.500.

## What this means

Negative result, defensible:

> Under controlled rendering (matched structure, matched observations),
> gpt-oss-20b does not discriminate correct from near-miss tool-use
> trajectories in the fam14 task family. Discrimination = 0.500,
> 95% CI [0.406, 0.594], n=32 comparisons.

Combined with the session's other finding (no hull in fam14 — 12/12
items in the "outside" family pass at least once across two runs),
the project's honest state is:

> No measurable AGI-adjacent capacity on fam14 for gpt-oss-20b.
> Two candidate signals were found and both turned out to be
> measurement artifacts. The infrastructure to make future
> measurements trustworthy is now in place.

## Open questions (v3)

1. SwapArgument was a no-op — args are empty in checkpoints.
   Capturing real args would activate the 5th mutation.
2. n=32 is small. A weak signal (0.55) would need n>=100 per family.
3. Undecided rate is ~60% — grammar-constrained probe (force
   single-char via server grammar) would tighten this.
4. Other families (fam13, fam11, distribution test) not probed.
5. Other models (120B tier) — if larger model discriminates, the
   null becomes a capability bound rather than a property of
   tool-using models.
